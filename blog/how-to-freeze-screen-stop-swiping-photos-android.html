01Why Android's Built-in "App Pinning" Falls Short

Your camera roll holds private moments, banking screenshots, and personal messages. Yet when you hand over your phone to show one vacation picture or QR ticket, muscle memory makes people instinctively swipe left or right.

Android's built-in App Pinning (Settings > Security) only stops someone from leaving an app. Inside Google Photos or Samsung Gallery, every swipe, pinch, and delete button stays completely active—and turning it on takes several clumsy taps through Recent Apps.

End "The Awkward Hover"

Without a touch lock, the only workaround is holding your phone with a death-grip while tilting it toward someone's face. A real touch freeze lets you hand your phone over relaxed and worry-free.

06Frequently Asked Questions

How do you lock your Android screen on one photo so people can't swipe?

With Swipe Stop, tap the freeze tile before handing your phone over. All touch interactions—swiping, tapping, and back gestures—are disabled while the photo remains visible. Unlock it with your fingerprint when you take it back.

Does Android have an equivalent to iOS Guided Access?

Stock Android's App Pinning only keeps someone inside an app—it still lets them swipe photos and press buttons. Swipe Stop fills that gap by disabling all touch input until you authenticate.

What if someone presses the physical power button while frozen?

Pressing the power button puts the phone into standard Android lock mode. Your regular lock screen security (PIN, fingerprint, or password) is required to unlock it again.

Does Swipe Stop have ads, and which Android phones are supported?

Swipe Stop has zero ads, works completely offline, and runs on Samsung, Google Pixel, Xiaomi, Motorola, and other devices on Android 9 through Android 15+.
