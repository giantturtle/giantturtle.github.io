1. The Universal "Photo Sharing Anxiety"

Modern smartphones hold the most intimate records of our lives: medical lab results, banking statements, personal conversations, relationship moments, and candid screenshots.

Yet every week, we find ourselves handing our unlocked devices to other people:

  • Showing a vacation picture to a colleague at work.
  • Showing a funny meme or dog video to your parents.
  • Passing your phone to a barista or ticket collector to scan a QR code or barcode.
  • Handing your phone to a child in the car to watch a cartoon.

Almost universally, human curiosity (or sheer accidental muscle memory) causes people to swipe left or right. Suddenly, someone you barely know is looking at private photos you never intended to share.

The Awkward Hover

Up until now, the only defense has been "the awkward hover"—holding your phone with a death-grip while tilting it toward your friend's face so their fingers cannot touch the glass. It feels antisocial, awkward, and uncomfortable.

2. Why Android's Built-in "App Pinning" Falls Short

Android has a built-in feature in Settings > Security called App Pinning (Screen Pinning). While useful for preventing someone from leaving an app, it does not solve the photo privacy problem:

  • App Pinning prevents the user from going back to the home screen, but all touch gestures inside the app remain completely active!
  • A pinned Google Photos or Samsung Gallery app still allows the viewer to swipe through every single photo, delete albums, or open shared memories.
  • Setting up App Pinning is clumsy, requiring navigating into the Recent Apps overview and tapping small submenu icons.
