01Why Stock Android & DeX Leave Most Phones Behind

Modern Android phones ship with 8-core Snapdragon, Tensor, or Dimensity chips and 8–16GB of fast RAM—plenty for writing, spreadsheets, coding, and research. Yet turning a standard phone into a workstation hits three walls:

Black Pillar Bars

Default screen mirroring projects a narrow 9:16 portrait strip onto a 16:9 monitor, wasting over half the display.

Stretched Launchers

Standard mobile launchers stretch widgets awkwardly in landscape and lack a desktop dock built for keyboard and mouse.

Locked Ecosystems

Samsung DeX and Moto Ready For are locked to flagship models—leaving Pixel, Xiaomi, OnePlus, and mid-range users out.

04Choosing Your Edition: Dextop vs. Dextop Core

Some manufacturers—especially Xiaomi (HyperOS / MIUI) and certain carrier-locked devices—restrict third-party launchers or disable full-screen navigation gestures when you change the default launcher. That's exactly why Dextop Core exists: it delivers the complete horizontal desktop workspace as a standalone app without replacing your home-screen launcher.

07Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Dextop and Dextop Core?

Dextop is a full standalone desktop launcher. Dextop Core delivers the same native landscape desktop workspace (themes, widgets, dock, keyboard support, casting) without replacing your default home launcher—making it ideal for Xiaomi HyperOS and phones where third-party launchers break full-screen gestures.

Can I get a Samsung DeX experience on Google Pixel, Xiaomi, or Motorola?

Yes. While Samsung DeX is restricted to high-end Galaxy phones, Dextop and Dextop Core work across Pixel, Xiaomi, Motorola, OnePlus, Nothing Phone, and virtually any Android smartphone or tablet.

Do I need an expensive USB-C dock or monitor to use Dextop or Dextop Core?

No. Both work standalone directly on your phone, tablet, or foldable screen. To go big-screen, cast wirelessly with Google Home on supported devices or plug in an inexpensive $15–$25 USB-C to HDMI adapter.

Do Dextop or Dextop Core require root access or ADB commands?

No. Both install like standard apps from the Google Play Store—zero root privileges, bootloader unlocks, or ADB scripts required.

Will either edition change my existing portrait home screen?

No. Both apps keep an independent landscape workspace with separate shortcuts and widgets, leaving your normal portrait home screen untouched.
