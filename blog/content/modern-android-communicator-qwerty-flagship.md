## 1. The E91 Legacy: The Last Communicator We Loved

The Nokia E91 was not trying to be a gaming console or a cinema. It was trying to be a **computer that fits in a jacket pocket**. Open it and you got a full QWERTY keyboard, a wide landscape screen, and a mail client that actually worked. Close it and it became a small, tough, all-day phone with a proper keypad.

Its big sibling, the E90 Communicator, went further: a clamshell that opened into a 4:3 landscape display the size of a small laptop, with a modifier row, arrow keys, and a navigation pad. Journalists, sysadmins, lawyers, and developers carried them for years.

Then the industry decided the future was a single sheet of glass, and physical keyboards were declared dead.

---

## 2. What We Lost When the Slate Won

- **Thumb typing caps out.** On-screen QWERTY is fine for "ok" and useless for a 600-word email.
- **The screen is the wrong shape.** A 20:9 portrait display is built for vertical scrolling, not documents, terminals, or spreadsheets.
- **No tactile anchors.** Physical modifiers, arrow keys, and Escape are what make keyboard-driven work fast.
- **The phone will not stay put.** A slate on a desk is a notification mirror; a clamshell lies flat at a natural angle.

---

## 3. The Modern Communicator Form Factor

Two states, one device, no compromises:

- **Closed:** ~6.1" portrait screen + portrait QWERTY in two thumb clusters.
- **Open:** ~7.8" landscape OLED display (16:10 or 4:3, ~120Hz) + full landscape keyboard with F-row, arrow cluster, and hardware modifiers.
- **Cable out:** USB-C with DisplayPort alt mode, Bluetooth 6, Wi-Fi 7. One cable to a monitor, mouse, and keyboard.
- **Endurance:** 5,000mAh+ battery in a roomier clamshell chassis, no fragile inner foldable panel.

You do not have to wait for an OEM. The software half of the communicator experience exists today:

**Tip:** enable windowed/freeform mode in Android developer options before connecting a keyboard or monitor.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions

### Would a modern communicator with a physical keyboard actually sell?
The niche is smaller than the slab market but loyal and underserved. Nokia sold millions of Eseries communicators, Unihertz kept the category alive, and foldables proved people pay a premium for better keyboard use cases.

### Can Android deliver a desktop-like experience on a phone keyboard?
Yes. Android already handles landscape orientation, USB/Bluetooth keyboards, multi-window, and external displays. Dextop and Dextop Core supply the missing desktop-class shell.

### Is this not exactly what a foldable does?
No. A foldable gives a big screen with no keyboard; a communicator bakes the keyboard into the chassis and avoids a flexible inner panel that creases and costs a fortune to replace.

### Do I need an external monitor?
No. A landscape communicator screen is already desktop-shaped. A USB-C dock or cast simply extends it into a full workstation.
