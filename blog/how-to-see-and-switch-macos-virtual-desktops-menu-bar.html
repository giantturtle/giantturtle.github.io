1. The Problem with Default macOS Spaces

macOS Spaces (Apple's implementation of virtual desktops) is one of the most powerful multitasking features on the Mac. Whether you are working on a MacBook Neo, MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, or an external workstation, dividing your work across multiple virtual desktops lets you keep code editors, communication tools, design canvases, and research tabs cleanly organized—even without external displays.

However, macOS has a frustrating design oversight that has bugged power users for over a decade: macOS never tells you which Space you are currently on, or what is sitting on the next and previous screens.

Once you switch to a desktop, there is zero persistent visual feedback in the menu bar or Dock indicating whether you are on Desktop 1, Desktop 3, or Desktop 5. You are forced to rely on memory or constantly trigger Mission Control just to re-orient yourself.

The "Context Blindness" Tax

Studies on developer and knowledge worker productivity indicate that micro-interruptions—such as bringing up Mission Control to check where a browser window lives—break cognitive flow state and cost up to 15 to 25 minutes of regained focus every day.

2. Why Native Gestures and Shortcuts Cause Fatigue

Apple gives you three default ways to navigate between virtual desktops, but each comes with serious ergonomic drawbacks:

  • Three- or Four-Finger Trackpad Swipes: Smooth on a laptop trackpad, but repetitive swiping across an 8-hour workday causes fatigue—and if you use an external mouse at a desk, horizontal switching is clunky or requires custom button mapping.
  • Ctrl + Left/Right Arrow: Sequential navigation means jumping from Desktop 1 to Desktop 4 requires pressing the shortcut three times in a row and sitting through three full-screen sliding animations.
  • Ctrl + Number Keys: Apple allows mapping Ctrl + 1, Ctrl + 2, etc., in System Settings > Keyboard > Keyboard Shortcuts > Mission Control. Even then, you still have no visual cue showing which number holds which apps.

4. Step-by-Step Setup Tutorial

Getting DesktopPlus running on your Mac takes less than 60 seconds:

1

Download and Install the Apple-Notarized Disk Image

Download the latest release of DesktopPlus (.dmg) (macOS 15.0 or later). DesktopPlus is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it installs cleanly—just open the DMG and drag DesktopPlus.app into your /Applications folder.

2

Launch & Grant Accessibility Permissions

Open DesktopPlus from Spotlight (Cmd + Space) or Applications. When prompted, grant Accessibility permission under System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility so DesktopPlus can detect and switch Spaces safely without modifying system files.

3

Pick Your Style & Enable Launch at Login

Your active desktops appear immediately in your menu bar. Click any space indicator to jump straight to it, and enable Launch DesktopPlus at login in Preferences so it starts silently every time you log in.

5. Customizing Display Styles & Preferences

Every workspace has its own visual rhythm. DesktopPlus includes four carefully crafted display modes so your menu bar indicator blends naturally into macOS:

Rectangles Numbers Rectangles + Numbers Opened Apps
  • Rectangles: Clean, minimal rectangular tiles that clearly highlight your active Space while keeping your menu bar distraction-free.
  • Numbers: Compact numeric indicators (1, 2, 3…)—great when you want minimal horizontal width around the MacBook camera notch.
  • Rectangles + Numbers: Combines framed desktop boxes with crisp space numbers so you can scan desktop positions instantly.
  • Opened Apps: Shows the icons of open applications right inside each desktop box, letting you see at a glance what is running on your current, previous, and next Spaces before switching.

Beyond display modes, the Preferences panel lets you fine-tune appearance and behavior without clutter: pick a custom Active space color, adjust Border opacity for more pronounced outlines, enable Wider desktop boxes, toggle the Hot zone popup, or even enable Move window to desktop when dragged to menu bar.

Pro-Tip: Preventing macOS from Rearranging Your Spaces

By default, macOS dynamically reorders your virtual desktops based on most recent use, which disrupts spatial muscle memory. To lock your Spaces in permanent order, open System Settings > Desktop & Dock, scroll to Mission Control, and toggle OFF "Automatically rearrange Spaces based on most recent use".
