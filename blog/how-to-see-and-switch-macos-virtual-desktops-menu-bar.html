01Why Default macOS Spaces Breaks Flow

Virtual desktops (Spaces) are fantastic for separating code editors, browsers, chats, and design apps on any MacBook or external monitor. Yet Apple leaves out one crucial detail: macOS never tells you which Space you're on—or what's sitting on adjacent desktops.

Swipe Fatigue

Three- or four-finger trackpad swipes get tiring over an 8-hour day, and feel clunky with an external desk mouse.

Sequential Sliding

Ctrl + arrows force you to step through every Space in between—watching three animations just to jump from Desktop 1 to 4.

Context Blindness

Even with Ctrl + 1 shortcuts mapped, there's zero visual cue showing which number holds which apps until you trigger Mission Control.

Pro-Tip: Lock Your Space Order

By default, macOS shuffles your Spaces by most recent use. Open System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Mission Control and toggle OFF "Automatically rearrange Spaces based on most recent use" to preserve muscle memory.

Can you see which macOS Space you are on without opening Mission Control?

Apple doesn't include a menu bar Space indicator. With DesktopPlus, your active virtual desktops remain visible in the menu bar as Rectangles, Numbers, Rectangles + Numbers, or Opened Apps icons.

Workspace App Presets let you snapshot your currently open apps into named presets and relaunch complete multi-app workflows with a single click whenever you switch projects.
